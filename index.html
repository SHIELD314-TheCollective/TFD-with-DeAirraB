A Note on the Process
"Fraud is never a neat straight line. Neither is the path or the process."

One direct hit out of twelve. That number deserves to be said plainly, not softened. The 30-day forward signals are where this publication takes analytical risk — and this audit is what owning that risk actually looks like.

The verified events in every roundup came from real FinCEN alerts, real DOJ press releases, real INTERPOL operations, and real court filings. That foundation holds. The typology analysis holds. The compliance action items are defensible. None of that changed.

What this audit surfaced: fraud trends don't follow a publishing calendar. The hospice surge prediction was grounded in real intelligence — CMS had already blocked the entry point before the call was made, and that information wasn't in the research window. The AI automation of scam centers is a documented emerging risk — the evidence just isn't there yet for a specific October event. Three signals materialized, two months late. That's not nothing. But it's also not what was said.

Going forward, the signals will be labeled for what they are: directional intelligence grounded in the month's verified events, with an honest horizon. Not a guarantee. Not a forecast to build operations around. A signal to watch and verify before acting on it.

That's the deal. Accountability is part of the process.
